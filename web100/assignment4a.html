One of the activities I worked on was about Understanding Network Devices, and it honestly helped me in clearing up some confusion on certain aspects on network devices. For example, how each device play different parts/rolls when it comes to communicating with eachother, I kind of thought routers, switches, and modems were all just different names for the same thing. However, after going through the lesson, I now learned that each of these devices plays a different role in how data travels through a network. Routers connect different networks and help data find the best route, while switches help devices within the same network communicate more efficiently. Hubs, on the other hand, are older and not as smart—they just send data to every device, which can slow things down. This was super eye-opening because I never realized how much goes into something as simple as using Wi-Fi at home or school. In the future, especially if I end up working remotely or in a tech-focused space, I can see this knowledge being really helpful in managing or troubleshooting my own network. It’s also just nice to finally understand how all the pieces fit together. The second activity I completed was on IP Addressing and Subnetting, and while it was a little more technical, I actually found it pretty interesting and helped in clarifying certain things. In a way I knew what the purpose of IP addresses were about and not just a set of random numbers, but now I have a better understanding that they’re part of a whole system that helps devices find and talk to each other. The lesson explained the differences between IPv4 and IPv6 and how subnetting works to keep networks organized and running smoothly. It was cool to see how subnetting helps control data traffic and improves security by keeping things more contained. This isn’t just something that IT people need to know—honestly, in today’s world, where everything is connected to the internet, having a basic understanding of how this stuff works is useful for anyone. Whether I’m managing smart devices, collaborating in online workspaces, or even helping troubleshoot a network issue, I know this knowledge will come in handy. In conclusion, these two activities gave me a much better understanding and clarification of the systems that make the internet work behind the scenes. It’s one of those things that not only I but most people don’t really think about until you learn it or are in someway a part of it, such as working in IT. Now that I know more about it, I feel more confident in understanding how the internet works and able to talk about networking in everyday situations.
