The first activity on this subject that I completed was focused on Password Strength and Security, and it really was a good refresher as to why and how vulnerable weak passwords can make us. I had a good understanding that just adding a capital letter and a couple of numbers to my password was just not enough in this age and time, this activity showed how easy it is for hackers to crack simple combinations using brute-force attacks or dictionary-based methods. This activity reiterated that longer and more complex passwords with a mix of letters, numbers, and special characters are much harder to break, especially if they’re unique to each account. It also reniforced me the idea of using passphrases instead of short passwords, which I found to be a great balance between security and memorability, which is something that I've been doing so for long time so it's nice to know that something that I've been doing is also recommended by online security firms. This is something I know I can apply in both my personal and professional life. Whether I’m securing school accounts, banking apps, or work-related tools in the future, having strong, unique passwords—and possibly using a password manager—will help protect my personal information and limit my exposure to cyber threats. The second activity I explored was about Phishing and Social Engineering, which covered how attackers use manipulation instead of technical hacks to steal sensitive information. I had heard and known about phishing scams before and thankfully I've never been a victim of one, but I didn’t realize how sophisticated and personalized some of these attacks can really be. The activity explained how cybercriminals can craft emails or fake login pages that look completely legit, tricking users into giving up login credentials, credit card info, or even access to entire systems. What stood out most to me was how often people fall for these schemes simply because they’re unaware or in a rush. I learned to always check email addresses carefully, avoid clicking on suspicious links, and never provide personal info unless I’m 100% sure the source is legit. As someone planning to work in a creative or digital field, I can absolutely see the importance of recognizing social engineering tactics, especially since so many jobs involve constant email communication and account management. Knowing how to spot phishing attempts can help me avoid compromising not only my own data but also my employer’s systems and clients’ information. In conclusion, these activities have not only refreshed my memory on certain things, but also gave a better understand just how important cybersecurity really is. It’s not just about fancy firewalls or antivirus software—it’s about building better habits and staying alert to both obvious and subtle threats.
