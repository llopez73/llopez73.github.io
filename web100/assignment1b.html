While completing the “What is the Internet?” activity from the Wikiversity Internet Fundamentals lesson, I was able to grasp a clearer, more technical definition of something I use every single day. I learned that the internet is a global network made up of countless interconnected computers, servers, routers, and devices. These components communicate using standardized protocols like TCP/IP, allowing data to travel in small packets through various routes to reach its destination. One aspect that really stood out to me was how the internet is designed with redundancy—meaning it can still function even when parts of it go offline or get overloaded. Before this activity, I viewed the internet as a simple tool for accessing websites and apps, but now I understand its complexity and resilience. This learning experience helps me appreciate the engineering that powers everything from emails to video calls. As a college student who’s constantly online for coursework and research, understanding how the internet actually works has helped me think more critically about network speed, data privacy, and security. In the future, especially if I enter a field that blends creativity and tech, like UI/UX design or digital marketing, this foundational knowledge will help me communicate more effectively with IT teams or web developers.!
